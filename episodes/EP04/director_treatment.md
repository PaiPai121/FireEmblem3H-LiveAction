# EP4 Director Treatment — 门后的仪式

## Visual thesis

The camera starts inside an ordered world and gradually loses permission to look. The first movement is ceremonial and frontal; the tomb introduces lateral obstruction and negative space; the aftermath returns to symmetry but no longer feels safe; the forest scene turns the frame red and oblique; the final coda removes institutional scale entirely.

## Palette and light

- Ceremony: pale stone, candle gold, desaturated blue-green shadows.
- Tomb: wet black stone, cyan flame, tarnished silver, narrow pools of practical light.
- Aftermath: warm cathedral gold with cold faces; the warmth belongs to the room, not the people.
- Forest conspiracy: rust red, charcoal, damp green, small controlled backlight around armor edges.
- Sothis coda: low-contrast green-white particles in a dark hall; no fantasy glow bloom that erases skin texture.

## Camera grammar

- Establish geography before close emotion. For the tomb and combat, every cut must preserve an intelligible north-side axis.
- Use slow dolly or restrained shoulder movement for institution scenes; use one deliberate handheld correction for the Death Knight reveal, not constant shake.
- Keep named academy characters in three-quarter or profile when a face card is not yet full-body approved. Avoid lineup poses and poster hero shots.
- START/KEY/END must describe a physical state change, not three lighting variations. Every prompt includes feet, contact shadows, prop count, and the handoff to the next shot.

## Performance direction

Byleth speaks rarely and makes space for others to act. Dimitri’s courtesy becomes a shield around the group. Dedue reads exits and distance before emotion. Rhea’s calm is an instrument of power. Manuela and Hanneman report facts that do not fit comfortably together. The Flame Emperor never rushes to explain. Sothis asks the one question nobody in the institution will ask aloud.

## Editorial spine

The cut pattern is: order → obstruction → impact → administrative calm → conspiratorial red → private uncertainty. Bell and stone are recurring sound bridges. Let the Death Knight’s first appearance arrive half a beat after the audience notices the wrong shadow. Let the forest cut begin on an unresolved metallic resonance, not a triumphant sting.

## Hard rejects

No game UI, tactical grid, dialogue box, screenshot border, watermark, anime/CGI render, generic villain, child-coded academy student, war-phase aging, gore, floating weapons, unexplained teleportation, axis flip, or dialogue repeat. Do not let the red forest scene turn into a fantasy poster or let Rhea become openly cartoonishly evil.
