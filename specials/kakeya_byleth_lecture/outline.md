# Outline: Byleth Teaches the 2D Needle Problem

Project ID: `KAK-BYLETH-LECTURE`
Stage: writing
Status: candidate_generated

## Format

One 7.5-8.5 minute cinematic educational short with live-action fantasy lecture framing and animated mathematical overlays.

## Act Structure

### Act 1: The Training Puzzle

Female Byleth places a unit-length wooden practice sword on the academy training ground and asks how little ground is needed to rotate it through all directions. Students offer intuitive answers: a circle, a triangle, a clever curve. The problem is framed as a tactical puzzle, not yet named.

Turn: The audience thinks this is an optimization puzzle with a small but positive answer.

### Act 2: Beautiful Candidate, Then Convex Truth

Female Byleth draws the deltoid and shows how a tangent chord can act as the unit needle. The deltoid area `pi/8` feels like the answer. Then Female Byleth changes the rule: if the region must be convex, the deltoid is illegal, and the best answer is an equilateral triangle of height 1.

Turn: The audience learns that constraints determine the answer; convex and non-convex worlds behave differently.

### Act 3: The Besicovitch Reversal

Female Byleth cuts a triangular direction fan into thin triangles, slides them, overlaps them, then repeats. The students object: did we lose directions? Female Byleth answers by tracking the colored direction bands. The area decreases while the direction coverage remains.

Turn: The original "minimum area" intuition fails. There is no smallest positive area.

### Act 4: Why This Matters

The lesson moves from physical turning to modern thin-tube thinking. Female Byleth thickens lines into `delta`-tubes and compares overlap at different angles. The video explains that this method leads to dimension results and informs analysis of many-direction thin structures.

Turn: The object of study is no longer the sword but how many directions can overlap in space.

### Act 5: Reveal and Close

Female Byleth finally names it: the Kakeya needle problem. The closing image returns to the training sword, now surrounded by faint ghost-lines of all directions.

Final idea: "The needle is small. The direction structure is not."

## Core Lines

- "We are not asking how far the sword can reach. We are asking how little ground can remember every direction."
- "Area was spent here. Direction was preserved here."
- "Cutting the fan does not cut the directions. Sliding the pieces does not rotate them."
- "This is not a trick about swords. It is a warning about many-direction geometry."
- "It is called the Kakeya needle problem."

## Production Risks

- The mathematical visuals may become too ornamental; every overlay must prove a point.
- Female Byleth may drift into a generic lecturer or ordinary student; keep tactical restraint and minimal words.
- The students can add clarity but must not turn the piece into comedy.
- The final "impact" section must not overclaim practical applications.
